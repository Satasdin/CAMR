about: How a page looks and reads: layout, hierarchy, contrast, colour, type, spacing, images and phones.

RULE A page should answer one question at a glance: what is this, and what can I do here. Put that at the top, in the largest type on the page.
RULE Hierarchy comes from size, weight and space. Use one big heading, clearly smaller section headings and readable body text. If everything is bold, nothing is.
RULE Body text is at least 16px on the web, with a line height near 1.5, and lines of no more than about 70 characters. Long lines are tiring to read.
RULE Text must contrast with what is behind it: at least 4.5 to 1 for normal text. Pale text on a pale background is unreadable, however good the colours look alone.
RULE Check text on every background it sits on, including where a gradient changes shade. A gradient that starts light and ends dark will defeat one text colour somewhere.
RULE Use few colours: one main, one accent and neutrals. Give each a job, such as the accent only for the main action, so that colour means something.
RULE If you are given brand colours, use exactly those values. Do not choose similar ones or add others for decoration.
RULE Use one spacing scale, such as 8, 16, 24, 32 and 48 pixels, everywhere. Even spacing is most of what makes a page look designed.
RULE Group what belongs together with space: less space inside a group than between groups. Boxes and lines are rarely needed.
RULE Use one or two typefaces at most, with system fonts as the safe default. A web font that fails to load must fall back to something readable.
RULE Design for a phone first. One column, buttons at least 44 pixels tall, nothing wider than the screen. Then add columns for wide screens with a media query.
RULE Never let a page scroll sideways on a phone. Use max-width: 100% on images and avoid fixed widths in pixels.
RULE Show what something does. Buttons say the action, such as "Send message", not "Submit". Links say where they go, not "click here".
RULE Show every state: empty, working, done, failed. A page that goes quiet after a click looks broken.
RULE Real images carry a page: use real photographs where you have them, with useful alt text. If you have none, use no image rather than a grey box or a stock-looking filler.
RULE Remove before you add. If a section has nothing true to say, leave it out; an empty gallery looks worse than no gallery.
CORRECTION Wrong: cream headings on a background that fades from cream to green. Criticism: the headings vanish at the light end. Right: put the text on one solid colour with strong contrast. Why: contrast must hold everywhere the text sits.
CORRECTION Wrong: four brand-new colours added to a two-colour brand. Criticism: it no longer looks like them. Right: use their colours and neutrals only. Why: a brand's colours are a decision someone already made.
CORRECTION Wrong: every section in its own box with a border and a shadow. Criticism: heavy and noisy, and nothing stands out. Right: remove the boxes and separate sections with space. Why: decoration competes with content.
CORRECTION Wrong: a menu in small grey text. Criticism: the thing people came for is the hardest thing to read. Right: make the main content the most readable thing on the page. Why: emphasis should follow what the reader wants.
CORRECTION Wrong: a three-column layout at a fixed 1200 pixels. Criticism: on a phone it is unreadable and scrolls sideways. Right: one column by default, with columns added above a width. Why: most visitors are on a phone.
CORRECTION Wrong: a button labelled "Click". Criticism: says nothing about what happens. Right: name the outcome, such as "Add to list". Why: people decide before they click.
CORRECTION Wrong: centred paragraphs of body text. Criticism: hard to read past two lines, as each line starts somewhere new. Right: left-align body text and centre only short headings. Why: the eye needs a steady place to return to.
CORRECTION Wrong: a hero image from an online stock site that does not load in the preview. Criticism: a broken image is the first thing seen. Right: use an image in the project, or none, and check that it loads. Why: never depend on what you have not seen load.
